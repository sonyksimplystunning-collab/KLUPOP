About the programme

What is a Professor of Practice?

A Professor of Practice (POP) is a distinguished professional from industry, government or public service who joins the university to teach and mentor students, without the conventional academic requirements of a Ph.D. or research publications. The value they bring is field experience: how technology is actually built, delivered, secured and managed.

The role follows the UGC guidelines for engaging Professors of Practice in higher education institutions. Experts with at least 15 years of service or professional experience in their field are eligible, and a POP is engaged for a fixed term rather than as a regular faculty post.

At KL University, the POP programme is run through the Alumni Relations office. Most of our POPs are KLU alumni now working at global companies, who return to teach subjects in the B.Tech, BBA and MBA programmes.

Glossary

Key terms

Professor of Practice (POP)
An industry or public-service expert engaged by the university to teach, co-teach and mentor, based on professional experience rather than academic credentials.
Subject mapping
Assigning each POP to a course in the curriculum that matches their working expertise, such as AI, Project Delivery or Analog VLSI Design.
Roadmap
The proposal a POP candidate submits: the subjects they can handle and the industry-gap add-ons they would offer.
Industry gap add-ons
Modules, workshops or certifications that bridge where graduates fall short when they join industry.
Project-based delivery
Courses taught through hands-on projects rather than lectures alone, for practical and interactive learning.
Engagement categories
Under UGC guidelines a POP can be industry-funded, funded by the institution's own resources, or engaged on an honorary basis.
Tenure
A fixed-term engagement, initially up to one year and extendable based on the POP's contribution, within the limit set by UGC.
Co-teaching
A POP delivers sessions alongside the regular course faculty, who handles assessment and academic records.
POP Cell
The office that sources and onboards POPs, schedules their sessions with timetable in-charges, collects student feedback and handles logistics.
Alumni POP
A KLU graduate who returns to campus as a Professor of Practice, linking alumni relations with teaching.

What POPs do on campus

Activities

  • Course deliveryTeaching or co-teaching the mapped subject, in person or online, with real case studies from their organisations.
  • Curriculum inputReviewing syllabi and suggesting updates so course content matches current industry tools and practice.
  • Projects and capstonesGuiding student projects, hackathon teams and final-year capstones on industry problem statements.
  • MentorshipOne-to-one and small-group mentoring on careers, higher studies and global opportunities.
  • Workshops and expert talksHands-on sessions on tools and technologies such as cloud, Gen AI, data engineering and cybersecurity.
  • Internships and placementsOpening doors to internships, mock interviews and recruitment connections with their companies.
  • Patents and innovationSupporting student and faculty ideas toward patent filings, prototypes and startups.
  • Faculty developmentSharing industry practice with faculty through FDPs and joint research or consultancy.

Joining as a POP

Selection process

  1. Initial conversationThe candidate speaks with the POP programme manager about their background, expertise and interest in teaching.
  2. Curriculum reviewThe candidate goes through the B.Tech (Y25 and Y26), BBA and MBA curricula linked below.
  3. Roadmap submissionThe candidate submits a roadmap listing the subjects they can teach, co-teach or support through guest lectures, labs or projects, and the industry-gap add-ons (modules, workshops or certifications) they propose.
  4. Recruitment team interactionOnce the roadmap is received, an interaction is arranged with the university's Recruitment Team.
  5. Approvals and onboardingAfter approval, the POP is onboarded, mapped to subjects and scheduled into the timetable by the POP Cell.

Norms for POPs

Travel and accommodation

  • POPs based abroad: round-trip economy air tickets each semester, with accommodation at the campus guest house for a minimum stay of one week.
  • POPs based in India: round-trip economy air tickets each semester, with guest-house accommodation for a minimum stay of 2 days, 3 to 4 times during the semester.
  • During the campus stay: student and faculty development programmes, workshops or hands-on sessions for at least 4 and up to 6 hours a day.
  • An honorarium is paid as per university norms.

Common conditions

  • FDPs, seminars, workshops and hackathons: active participation in offline events during visits to campus.
  • Project-based delivery: courses are delivered through projects for practical, interactive learning.
  • Guidance and advising: guiding and mentoring students through the delivery of their projects.
  • Weekly connections: weekly sessions with students, offline or online, of 2 hours each in a single stretch.
  • Total hours: 50 to 55 hours per semester per course across these activities.
